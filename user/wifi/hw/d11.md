# The 802.11 core, D11

The enumeration ROM puts the 802.11 core, D11, revision 39, at `0x18001000`, a page of registers, see `README.md`.
Its registers' names here are Linux's brcmsmac's, `struct d11regs` in `drivers/net/wireless/broadcom/brcm80211/brcmsmac/d11.h`,
**known** for the revisions brcmsmac drives and assumed for this one, whose layout no open source gives;
a name stands for the word that holds it, two 16-bit registers to a word where the layout has them so.

## What it offers

- `smpl_clct_stpptr` and `smpl_clct_curptr`, at `0x554`, are the pointers of the core's sample collection,
  which writes what the receiver's converters take, as samples, into the core's memory:
  the engine SEEMOO's Pair-Fi captures raw I/Q with on Broadcom's chips, as it publishes,
  and sends samples out through the PHY's sample play buffer the other way, **known**.
  brcmsmac starts one with `maccommand`'s bit 5, `MCMD_SAMPLECOLL`, the microcode taking it with a trigger and a type from its shared memory;
  this microcode does not take the bit alone, see "D11's memories".
- `phyregaddr` and `phyregdata`, at `0x3fc`, reach the PHY's registers, and `radioregaddr` and `radioregdata`, at `0x3d8`,
  the radio's, one at a time through an address and its data, which the WLAN firmware uses as well:
  a read from the host between the firmware's write of an address and its access of the data takes the firmware's place,
  so they are read only once the WLAN's processor is stopped, see `phy.md`.

## D11's memories

`user/wifi/tools/cyw43-regmap.py d11mem` reads them from the host, the WLAN's processor held in reset, **read**:
the shared memory, the microcode's and the scratch registers through `objaddr` and `objdata`, at `0x160`, as brcmsmac does,
`objaddr`'s read increment set so that each read of `objdata` gives the next word;
and the template memory through `tplatewrptr` and `tplatewrdata`, at `0x130`, whose pointer a read moves on by a word,
though brcmsmac only writes it.
Each memory reads again from its start past its size:

| Memory | Size | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| shared, `objaddr`'s select 1 | 8 KiB | 52 of its 2048 words differ between its copies, read one after another: the microcode's live state |
| microcode, select 0 | 64 KiB, 8192 instructions | the firmware's microcode, then words not the firmware's |
| scratch, select 2 | 64 words | 46 or 47 of them nonzero |
| template | 32 KiB through its window | almost every word nonzero |

The microcode is the RAM firmware's, **read**:
`43439A0.bin` holds at `0x2d298` a word of its length, 44170 bytes, and then 6310 instructions,
each of 50 bits in 7 bytes, the most significant first, which read from the microcode's memory a word pair each, the low word first;
the 1882 after them are none of the image's, what the memory powered up with, a hypothesis.
Its instructions are left to read, map step 3.

Sample collect, **measured**: with the WLAN's processor held in reset, its start and stop pointers set to the template's words `0x1000` and `0x1fff`

## Its processor, the PSM

The PSM's instructions are as "Rolling the D11" (Link, Breuer, Gringoli and Hollick, WiNTECH '23) describes them, **known**,
and as this microcode's own read, **read**:
an opcode from bit 39 and three operands of 13 bits, two read and one written,
each operand the shared memory's halfword, an internal register, IHR, the shared memory through one of the bases `psm_base_0` to `6`,
a scratch register or an immediate of 11 bits, by its value; `user/wifi/tools/cyw43psm.py` names them.
Its encodings of `or`, `orx`, `add`, `jnzx` and the jump on the condition always true
give what the microcode holds at `0x126`, `0x127`, `0x12c` and `0x62b`, **read**.

The microcode reaches the PHY's registers through the IHR `0x18` and `0x19`, **read**, its routines at `0x127` and `0x12b`:
a read writes the register or'ed with `0x6000` to `0x18`, waits for bit 14 of `0x18` to clear and reads the data from `0x19`;
a write puts the data in `0x19` and then the register or'ed with `0x4000` in `0x18`.

Our own microcode runs, **measured**: written into the microcode's memory from 0 through `objaddr`, the PSM held at 0 by `maccontrol`
with the internal registers on and wake, `0x04000404`, it read back whole, and started with `0x04000402` it ran.
A loop of an `add` to `psm_base_0` and a jump back took 3.000 of the WLAN processor's cycles, at its 81.6 MHz, in each of 31 spans of 8000:
the PSM runs on a clock locked to that processor's.
Each further plain instruction in a loop adds about one cycle, so the jump takes two, a hypothesis.
The microcode of `cyw43-iq.py --mode psm` takes the receiver's samples so, see `phy.md`;
the vendor's microcode is then gone, and D11 goes into reset at the end.

The PSM's own accesses of the PHY slow while the WLAN's processor reads D11's registers, **measured**:
the same loop of estimates took 63.12 cycles a sample with the processor reading `psm_base_0` as fast as it could,
62.68 with a slower loop of reads, and 61.99 with it reading only near the start and the end.
Once, the processor's write of 0 to `psm_base_0`, made with the PSM held, came after the microcode's first instruction had set it, **measured**:
the run's samples lay two halfwords on; the processor now leaves `psm_base_0` to the microcode.

### Its receive engine, from our microcode

The microcode puts a received frame's header into the receive FIFO from the shared memory, **read**, at `0xe89`:
the IHR `0x07` takes the header's first halfword, `0x08` how many halfwords to copy, and `0x04`'s bits 0 and 1 set to 1 start the copy;
d11-emu names them `RCV_SHM_STADDR`, `RCV_SHM_STCNT` and `RCV_CTL`.
Our microcode so makes frames of its own, which the receive DMA lays in RAM like any, **measured**:
each takes `RCV_STATUS_LEN`, the IHR `0x06`, bytes, its low 7 bits alone, so at most 126, while `0x07` steps on by them and `0x08` counts down,
a push of more halfwords making several frames; a frame of 126 bytes is copied in about 80 cycles of the WLAN processor's.
The engine clears bits 8 to 11 of each frame's halfword 1, **measured**: `0xa501` landed as `0xa001`, `0x0790` as `0x0090`.
Pushed faster than the DMA empties the FIFO, the pushes stop for good, **measured**: after 59 to 73 frames, whatever their length,
a push stayed 6 halfwords in; paced at 880 cycles a push of 126 bytes, 11.7 MB/s, 250 went through.

Taken over abruptly, the PSM halted while the vendor's microcode ran, the engine may be mid-frame, **measured**:
in some sessions, the same in all else, each push then brought the same 126 bytes of something not in the shared memory, or nothing.
Suspended first, as brcmsmac suspends the MAC, `maccontrol`'s enable cleared and `macintstatus`'s bit 0 awaited,
the vendor's microcode leaves it idle: in every session since, all pushes came whole.
That suspend also clears bits 6 and 7 of `BTCX_TRANSCTL`, the IHR `0x15c`, `0xc0` to 0, **measured**, among a few PHY and radio bits,
and with them the calibration's tones vanished from the receiver's samples; that register put back alone, they came back:
the WLAN gives the front end it shares with Bluetooth up, a hypothesis. `cyw43psm.suspend` puts it back.
